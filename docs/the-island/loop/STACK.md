# THE STACK — story spine v2 (canon 2026-08-13)

> **Supersedes the REVEAL of [SPINE.md](SPINE.md); keeps its texture, its ledger, and every
> shipped system.** SPINE v1 chose GRIEF → INTEGRATION and landed it as an identity twist
> ("you are the one the light was lit for"). The texture v1 built is excellent and stays.
> The *reveal* is replaced, because it was the one part of the game the machine wasn't
> needed for.
>
> **Canon guardrails, unchanged:** zero external deps (three.js only) · ALL-METAPHOR, no
> biography · power-safe 60fps · the 1:240 chart-table model mirrors the island · never
> literal · one WorldState.

---

## 1. The diagnosis (why v1 read as trite)

Three faults, in order of severity.

**(a) The story didn't need the machine.** ABYME's singular asset is *a diegetic simulation
whose state authors reality, nested at 1:240, arbitrarily deep*. Nobody else has that. v1
pointed it at mourning — which you can stage equally well with a house and a diary. The
mechanic is about **authorship, displacement, and who is turning the valve on whom.** The
story kept declining to be about that.

**(b) Nothing opposed the player.** No antagonist, no clock, no agenda, no possible loss.
Grief is a mood, not an opponent. Every beat guided the player toward an embrace at the
bottom, and the player could feel that from minute five. That is the *predictability*.

**(c) The twist stayed inside the genre's default move.** v1 correctly killed "you were the
keeper all along," then shipped "you are the *object* of the search" — the same class of
reveal (identity, retroactive, delivered by the ending rather than played).

---

## 2. The thesis — THE COST RUNS DOWNHILL

**The law of the stack: a solution is never dissolved, only displaced.**

Every act that makes your island easier makes the island one rung down harder. Drain your
sea and the water has to go somewhere: it goes *down*. The tide rising on you is not
weather and not metaphor — it is **somebody above you solving their puzzle.**

That is what the nesting was always for. The keeper did not build a smaller island out of
grief. He built it so he would not have to pay. Each new rung was a place to put the water.
And the moment you turn the brass valve, you are doing it too.

- **The antagonist** is the model itself — the most useful object in the game, and the
  instrument of the harm. Every verb is a temptation.
- **The clock** is the tide: monotonic, shared, never fully receding. What drowns stays
  drowned.
- **The stake** is a person you can affect and never meet.
- **The choice** is not *hug or don't*. It is: **pay it here, or pass it down.**

One line of canon, said aloud once, ever:

> *"It has to go somewhere."*

## 3. The four systems

### 3.1 THE LEDGER — the stack is inhabited *(the keystone)*

Each rung of the stack keeps an **append-only ledger of marks**: one row per act a hand
performed there. Your rung inherits the ledger of the rung above and renders it as
**evidence** — lampblack in a margin that isn't yours, a bridge already laid, a chest already
open, footprints from a route you didn't walk, the sea already at your knees.

- You never see another hand. You see **what they did to you.**
- The keeper's voice — already shipped, already the only "I/you" in the game — stops being a
  character and becomes **the hand below yours, calling up.** In the offline build that hand
  is a recording. Online it is a person.
- The reveal is no longer about identity. It is about **consequence**, and it is played, not
  narrated: the first time you realize the water you drained is standing in someone's study.

**Offline first, and it is not a fallback.** *You* are the hand one rung up. Your own L1
marks are what L2 inherits. The mechanic ships single-player and complete; multiplayer is a
**data-source swap**, not a new game. (§8.)

### 3.2 THE DRAFT — the cost, as one number

A ship's *draft* is how much water it displaces. Yours is how much you push down.

Every mark carries a small **draft** cost. The draft of every rung above you sums into
**your** tide. Solve efficiently and the rung below inherits a puddle; brute-force the whole
chain and they inherit a flood — with the same island, the same puzzles, and less of it above
water.

This gives the game its missing quantity: **a way to be worse or better at it that has
nothing to do with skill.** The tide gauge's "fresh fifth ring" (already in the world,
already unbound in v1's foreshadow ledger) is now the readout for exactly this.

### 3.3 THE DRIFT — the model stops being trustworthy

v1 shipped *The Room That Disagrees* as a single framed vista. It is promoted to the spine.

The entire game teaches one law: **the model tells the truth about the world.** The drift is
that law failing — and it fails *for a reason*: the ledger above is editing your world while
you are not looking. A ruler laid on the crack that does not raise a bridge. A margin stroke
appearing between two glances. Weather through a window that contradicts your sky.

This is the dread register, and it is *earned* rather than atmospheric: the model is no
longer lying, it is **out of date**, because somebody upstream moved.

### 3.4 THE ERAS AS RULESETS — the chapters become worlds

The strata are already ERAS ([ERAS.md](ERAS.md)) and already carry a per-era color script.
That is currently a *grade*. Make it a **ruleset**: each era changes what the model does,
what the world permits, and what you are.

| Era | The rule that changes |
|---|---|
| **lastday** (L1) | Baseline. The model obeys. You are alone and the island is yours. |
| **arrival** (L2) | Wonder, and the first displacement. The model's edits land *late* — you see the cost arrive after you cause it. |
| **inspection** (L3) | Your ledger is **read**. Marks are filed, carried, or kept (`recDisp`, already shipped). Authority that measures instead of protects. The Watcher is the eye of it. |
| **lastwinter** (L4) | The model refuses some verbs entirely. The only working instrument is the plate. |

The era is not a paint job. It is *what kind of place this is to be a person in.*

## 4. What this re-reads (nothing shipped is wasted)

The single best property of this spine: it recontextualizes v1's world without asking for
one asset back.

| Shipped thing | v1 meaning | v2 meaning |
|---|---|---|
| The rising tide / SEA-STRATA | eras of a keeper's tenancy | **the accumulated draft of everyone above** |
| The brass valve | grief re-enacted | the god-verb: relief here, flood there |
| The keeper's voice from below | your mourned self | **a hand you are drowning** |
| *"Step off the plate, I'm begging you"* | don't doom yourself | **don't add another rung** |
| The Room That Disagrees | uncanny vignette | the ledger above, mid-edit |
| The Watcher (regard, don't flee) | integration rehearsal | unchanged — and now the *inspection* made flesh |
| The nested glint, "far down a light is still lit" | the keeper | **the rung below is occupied right now** |
| `recDisp` — carried / filed / kept | inspector flavor | the disposition of **your** marks |
| The tide gauge's unmet fifth ring | mood | the draft readout |
| The chart tally strokes | you went down | **hands, not levels** |
| The coat, the salt, the lamp oil | the reveal device | pure texture — and better for it |

The grief material **stays as texture and loses its job as the reveal.** That is the whole
edit. The warmth still wounds; it just isn't the answer to the mystery anymore.

## 5. The twist, replaced

v1: *you are the thing the search was for.* (Identity. Retroactive. Delivered.)

v2: **you are somebody's rung.**

The turn is not learning who you are. It is learning what you have been doing — and that it
was done to you by someone who also didn't know. Nobody in the stack is a villain. Everyone
is solving their island. **That** is the horror and the tenderness at once, and it is the
one shape the machine was built to deliver.

The keeper is not your shadow, your father, or your future. He is a person one rung down who
has been asking you, the entire game, to stop.

## 6. The endings — four, all valid, none punished

The plate is still the instrument. The question it asks is new.

1. **TEND** — stay on your rung and hold the water here. You solve nothing further; you also
   pass nothing down. The light stays lit for a stranger who may never come.
2. **CARRY** — go down and undo your own marks in their world. The only ending where you
   spend your remaining time on somebody else's island.
3. **OPEN** — leave your ledger open. Your solutions become theirs to inherit *and* their
   draft becomes yours. Connection, at cost, in both directions.
4. **CLOSE** — seal the plate. No one comes down after you. No one is helped either. Your
   peace is real, and it is smaller.

No ending fixes the stack. That is the point, and it is the honest one: **you cannot drain
the ocean, only decide where you stand in it.** The final image is unchanged across all
four — the same rung, the same lamp, golden hour, long shadows.

## 7. The architecture carried (ALL-METAPHOR — abstract structures only)

The emotional spine is drawn from the owner's private narrative design. Per the standing
guardrail, **no event, person, place, or company from it ever appears in this repo or the
build.** What crosses over is *structure*, stated universally:

- **Chapters are worlds with different rules**, not one continuous space with new paint.
  → §3.4.
- **Objects from your past gain power through significance, not crafting.** The things you
  find below are the things a hand left — and one rung down, they are *yours*. → the ledger
  makes this free and literal.
- **The mirror**: confront a fragment of a former self; *accept or reject*; acceptance is the
  upgrade. → leaning over the model and regarding the figure is already this gesture. Give
  it the verb.
- **Attacking the shadow feeds it; regard shrinks it.** → already shipped as the Watcher.
  Keep it as the game's only resolution verb, everywhere.
- **Being outnumbered and unseen** — resources consumed before you reach them, attention
  never landing on you. → one era where the ledger above is *crowded*: a hundred hands'
  marks over your island and none of them yours. (Online only; a genuine use of scale.)
- **Authority that measures instead of protects.** → the inspection era, already written and
  currently the strongest prose in the corpus. Promote it out of optional lore.
- **Power that costs you connection** — the instrument that solves everything and empties
  the room. → the model. This is the antagonist. → §2.
- **Integration over victory; the world stays broken; some gardens grow anyway.** → §6.
  Personal peace is a valid outcome even when the system is unfixed. No ending is scored.

The universal truth ABYME carries: **the darkness you are fighting is partly one you are
generating, and the work is to pay its cost yourself rather than pass it on.**

## 8. Multiplayer — the honest engineering

**There is no netcode, because there is no simultaneity.** Nobody is ever in your world at
the same time as you. Clients never reconcile. This is Dark Souls bloodstains, not a shooter.

- ABYME's world is deterministic (seed + WorldState + [save-schema.js](../js/save-schema.js)).
  A hand's whole existence compresses to **a list of marks**.
- The server is an append-only log behind two routes — `POST /strata/:rung/marks`,
  `GET /strata/:rung/marks?since=` — plus one integer for the shared tide.
- No lobbies, no sessions, no matchmaking, no tick rate, no interpolation, no lag
  compensation. A 400 ms round trip is invisible because nothing is live.
- Degrades to offline on the same code path: the ledger source is an interface, and the
  local implementation (your own past self) is the default.
- **Cost to build:** a Worker + KV, or ~80 lines of Node + SQLite. It is a weekend.

**The client never trusts the server's marks.** Inbound marks are schema-validated, capped
per rung, and clamped in draft before they touch WorldState — a hostile ledger must not be
able to flood a player's island or inject geometry. (Sanitation lives in `ledger.js`, not at
the call sites.)
