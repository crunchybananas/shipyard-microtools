# The Island — narrative spine

## The experience

A stranger finds a lighthouse with a working model of its own island. There is a
room behind the study: a blue blanket, two cups, a spare chair and a small boat with
a stitched side. The player learns to change the island, follows those changes
into the rooms below, then returns to decide what to keep doing.

The emotional direction is quiet, humane wonder. The strange geometry is large;
the human business is ordinary. People lose whole afternoons to work. Someone
brings a chair. A story is interrupted and can still be finished. A boat does not
have to be made again before it can be put in the water.

This is fiction. It contains no biographical names, places, employers, dates or
recognisable sequence of personal events. Keep private source material outside
runtime, art files, exports, tests and public documentation.

## The people

**The keeper** is competent and tired. He records things because recording has
helped him before. He begins to confuse keeping the light with doing everything
himself. His pages get less busy as he makes room for another person. He is allowed
to be funny, distracted, pleased, and wrong without being a moral lesson.

**The boatwright** has a life and a craft of her own. She notices cold tea, a broken
rung, the bird waiting for the music box. She offers company and practical changes.
She never delivers a diagnosis or an inspirational speech. Her invitations can be
accepted later. She does not exist to solve the keeper.

**The visitor** arrives with forms. Their private additions show what the official
language misses. The change is from copying a walking time to carrying wet papers
upstairs, not a villain becoming a saint.

The Tide-Figure, shore visitor and Lower Hand remain separate encounters. The
player's past is unassigned. There is no identity-reveal twist and no claim that
the player caused everything painful in the world.

## The journey

1. **Ashore.** Find the bottle and the light. Discover the room before mastering its machinery.
2. **A little command.** Light the refuge, turn the valve, lay the ruler and move the sun.
3. **A first crossing.** Go to the shallows. Experience another hand changing the water. Wait with the Tide-Figure.
4. **A first return.** The lamp is still lit. This is a genuine return, not the ending and not a reset.
5. **Listening.** Compare the imperfect music box with the bird. Follow the lens, cliff figures, instrument bindings, shadow and plumb into a deeper crossing.
6. **Other hands.** Read the register and visitor's pages. Stay with the shore figure. People have been doing this work without you.
7. **The unfinished boat.** At the source, read about the repeated sterns and the blue stitching. Regard the small figure without collecting or correcting it.
8. **Homecoming.** Select a physical water operation, ascend through every room, and return to the lamp. Optionally take the little boat and put it into the model sea. Its full-sized counterpart floats offshore.
9. **An evening after.** Commit at the refuge lamp. The world continues beyond the final image.

## The boat and the chair

The spare chair moves when pulled out, and its position survives Continue. The
note is read as a note, with ordinary page-based evidence rules.

After the final return, the boat can be carried from the room to the model sea.
One launch flag places the same hull geometry at both scales. Its stitched side
remains visible. It has no repair meter, grade, moral rank or completion percentage.
The launch is optional, available in all four endings, and persists on reload.

The boat is the metaphor. Do not append a sentence explaining what it stands for.

## Above and below

The keeper's watch book is found after climbing the real tower. He counts the
stairs; someone else remembers calling him down for soup. Neither gets the last
word about what the disagreement means.

In the western study beneath the bluff, the visitor describes three people saving
wet papers together. Names go into the bread tins first; the measurements can be
taken again. Opening the tin is an optional, persistent act. These discoveries
give the people more room without adding another required puzzle or an explanation
of the island.

## Language and evidence

Readable artifacts have particular authors. Technical measurements belong where
someone needed to make a measurement. Put the kettle, bag, socks, cup or unfinished
sentence on the page when that is what the person was attending to.

Short world cues report something visible or audible. Avoid telling the player
what the island knows, remembers, forgives, wants, or has decided. Allow silence.
Field Notes remain factual records of encountered material. Hints are explicitly
requested, separate from evidence, and never unlock a crossing by themselves.

The signal puzzle retains its physical deduction: beam order, instrument bindings,
then counts observed in the world. Story warmth does not make the books an answer key.

## Four endings

The index offers **hold**, **reverse**, **join** and **seal**. The final touch is at
the small refuge lamp. Each operation has a distinct physical result, camera,
waterline and coda. None is a verdict on the player's worth.

An ending may show work continuing, water returning, rooms joined, or a boundary
kept. Each leaves a human-scale possibility afterward. The east room remains a
place of rest, not a trap or a last-minute punishment.
